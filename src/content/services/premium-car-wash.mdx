faqs:
  - question: "How often should I wash my car in Dubai?"
    answer: "Every one to two weeks, and sooner after a sandstorm. Dubai dust is gritty rather than soft, so if it sits on the paint it gets rubbed in the next time anything touches the car, including your own hand. If you can only manage one thing, just rinse the car down with a hose. Plain water removes most of the abrasive dust before it has a chance to do any damage."
  - question: "Can I wash my car myself at home?"
    answer: "Yes, and it is worth doing if you enjoy it. You need two buckets, a hose, a proper car shampoo, a microfiber wash mitt and a couple of drying towels. Fill one bucket with soapy water and one with clean water, wash a panel at a time, and rinse the mitt in the clean bucket before it goes back into the soap. Work from the roof down so the dirtiest parts are last. That routine alone avoids most of the damage people accidentally do to their own paint."
  - question: "What is the most common mistake people make washing their own car?"
    answer: "Dish soap and a single bucket. Dish soap is designed to strip grease, so it also strips whatever protection is on your paint. One bucket means you are putting the dirt you just removed straight back onto the next panel. After those two, the most common mistake is washing in direct sun, which dries the shampoo onto the paint and leaves spots that are harder to remove than the dirt was."
  - question: "Should I wash my car in the sun?"
    answer: "Try not to. In Dubai the panels get hot enough that water and shampoo dry before you can rinse them off, which bakes mineral spots onto the surface. Early morning, evening, or any shaded spot is much better. If you have no choice, do one panel at a time and rinse it straight away rather than soaping the whole car first."
  - question: "Why does my car look swirled or scratched after a wash?"
    answer: "Those fine circular marks come from grit being dragged across the paint, not from the soap itself. It usually means dirt was still on the car when something touched it, or the mitt or towel had grit in it. Rinsing thoroughly before you touch the paint, using clean microfiber, and throwing away anything that falls on the floor are what prevent it. Dark cars show it most, but it happens on every colour."
  - question: "Are automatic car washes bad for the paint?"
    answer: "The brush type ones are, yes. They run the same bristles over your car that they ran over every car before yours, grit included, and that is what creates swirl marks. Touchless automatic washes are much safer for the paint, though they rely on strong chemicals and often leave the car less clean. If you use an automatic wash regularly, touchless is the better of the two."
  - question: "How do I safely remove bird droppings and tree sap?"
    answer: "Quickly and without scrubbing. Bird droppings are acidic and can etch into the clear coat within a day or two in Dubai heat, so the sooner they come off the better. Lay a damp cloth over the spot for a minute to soften it, then lift it away rather than rubbing at it. Never attack a dried one with a dry cloth, because that is grinding a hard lump across your paint."
  - question: "Will washing damage my ceramic coating or paint protection film?"
    answer: "No, washing regularly is exactly what keeps both working properly. What harms them is harsh detergent, brush style automatic washes, and aiming a pressure washer straight into the edges of film. Use a pH-neutral shampoo, keep some distance from film edges, and dry the car rather than letting it air dry in the sun. Looked after that way, both last as long as they should."
  - question: "Which wash level should I choose?"
    answer: "Level 1 keeps a clean car clean, so it suits regular upkeep between bigger jobs. Level 2 is the one most people book: the car is properly cleaned inside and out, including the engine bay and wheel arches, and you leave with two to three months of protection. Choose Level 3 when the paint feels rough or gritty after a wash. That roughness is dirt that has bonded to the surface, and it needs chemicals to lift rather than more soap."
  - question: "Why does an SUV cost more than a sedan?"
    answer: "It is a bigger car, so every step takes longer. More paint to wash, bigger wheels and arches, and more interior to vacuum. The difference is AED 20 on Level 1, AED 10 on Level 2 and AED 25 on Level 3. If you are not sure which applies to your car, send us the model and we will confirm before you book."
  - question: "How long does the protection last?"
    answer: "About two to three months on Level 2, and about six months on Level 3. It adds gloss and keeps the car cleaner between washes, so dirt rinses away more easily. It is not the same as a full ceramic coating, which is a separate service and lasts years. If your car already has a coating, this tops it up rather than replacing it."
